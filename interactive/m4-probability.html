Module 4 · Probability & Statistics

Probability quantifies uncertainty on a scale from 0 to 1. Statistics works in the opposite direction — from observed data back to the underlying mechanism.

4.1 Foundations

A sample space lists every possible outcome; an event is any subset of it. Everything follows from three axioms: probabilities are non-negative, the whole space has probability 1, and mutually exclusive events add.

Always consider the complement first. P(Ec) = 1 − P(E). Any question containing the phrase “at least one” is almost always easier as 1 − P(none).

Conditional probability

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)

Conditioning shrinks the sample space to B; you are re-measuring A inside that smaller world. That one sentence is the entire concept.

Independent ≠ mutually exclusive (trap #12). These are nearly opposites. Mutually exclusive events with non-zero probability are strongly dependent — if one happens, the other definitely did not.

Bayes' theorem

P(B | A) = P(A | B)·P(B) / Σ P(A | Bj)·P(Bj)

TermNameMeaning
P(B)Priorbelief before seeing evidence
P(A | B)Likelihoodhow well the hypothesis explains the evidence
P(B | A)Posteriorupdated belief after the evidence
denominatorEvidencenormaliser so posteriors sum to 1

Bayes reverses the direction of conditioning — it converts “probability of the symptom given the disease” into “probability of the disease given the symptom”. These two are wildly different numbers.

PROBLEM 4.2 · 2 marks

A disease affects 1% of a population. A test has 99% sensitivity and a 5% false-positive rate. Someone tests positive. What is the probability they have the disease?

Show full solution

Step 1 — the evidence, by total probability.

P(+) = (0.99)(0.01) + (0.05)(0.99) = 0.0099 + 0.0495 = 0.0594

Step 2 — Bayes.

P(D | +) = 0.0099 / 0.0594 = 1/6 ≈ 0.1667

(Computed in exact rational arithmetic.)

Why this is counter-intuitive. The test is 99% sensitive, yet a positive result means only a 1-in-6 chance of disease. In 10,000 people there are 100 sick (99 test positive) but 9,900 healthy (495 test positive anyway). The 495 false positives swamp the 99 true positives. The rarer the disease, the worse this gets. ∎

PROBLEMS 4.1 & 4.3 · 1 mark each

4.1 Two dice: find P(sum = 7) and P(sum = 7 | first die even). Independent?
4.3 A die is rolled 4 times. P(at least one six)?

Show solutions

4.1 Sum 7 happens 6 ways out of 36 → 1/6. Given the first die is even (18 outcomes), the favourable ones are (2,5), (4,3), (6,1) → 3/18 = 1/6. Equal, so independent. Intuition: whatever the first die shows, exactly one value of the second completes a 7. (Both machine-verified.)

4.3 Use the complement: P(no six once) = 5/6, so P(no six in four) = (5/6)⁴ = 625/1296. Therefore P(at least one) = 671/1296 ≈ 0.5177 (exact fraction verified). ∎

4.2 Random Variables

A random variable assigns a number to each outcome, converting outcomes into arithmetic. Discrete variables use a probability mass function; continuous ones use a density function.

For a continuous variable, P(X = c) = 0 at every single point. Probability lives in intervals. Density is not probability — it is probability per unit length, and it may exceed 1.

Var(X) = E[X²] − (E[X])²

Key properties: E[aX + b] = aE[X] + b but Var(aX + b) = a²Var(X) — adding a constant shifts the mean but not the spread. Also E[X+Y] = E[X]+E[Y] always, even for dependent variables, while variances add only when independent.

GATE names exactly five. Learn each by its generating story and you never have to guess which formula applies.

Memorylessness — the exponential's defining property

P(X > s + t | X > s) = P(X > t)

A component that has already survived s hours is exactly as good as new. The distribution has no concept of wear. This is unique to the exponential among continuous distributions.

PROBLEMS 4.4 – 4.8 · mixed

4.4 X ~ Binomial(10, 0.3): find P(X=3), P(X≤2), mean, variance.
4.5 Calls arrive Poisson at 2/min: find P(0), P(2), P(at least 1).
4.6 Bulb life exponential, mean 5 yr: P(>8), and P(>13 | already 5).
4.7 Marks ~ N(70, 8): what fraction score above 86?
4.8 X ~ Uniform(2, 10): mean, variance, P(X > 7).

Show all solutions

4.4 P(X=3) = C(10,3)(0.3)³(0.7)⁷ = 0.266828; P(X≤2) = 0.382783; mean = np = 3; variance = np(1−p) = 2.1. (All machine-computed; the full pmf was verified to sum to exactly 1.) Note P(X=3) is the largest single probability — which makes sense, since the mean is 3.

4.5 P(0) = e⁻² = 0.135335; P(2) = 2e⁻² = 0.270671; P(≥1) = 1 − 0.135335 = 0.864665. Note P(1) = P(2) exactly — this happens whenever λ = 2, since the ratio P(k)/P(k−1) = λ/k equals 1 at k = λ.

4.6 λ = 1/5 = 0.2, so P(X>8) = e⁻¹·⁶ = 0.201897. By memorylessness P(X>13 | X>5) = P(X>8) = 0.201897 — identical (both verified). The five years of service left no trace.

4.7 Z = (86 − 70)/8 = 2, so P = ½(1 − 0.9545) = 0.02275, about 2.3%. This matches the empirical rule: 4.55% lies outside ±2σ, and half of that is in the upper tail.

4.8 Mean = 6; variance = 64/12 = 5.3333; P(X>7) = 3/8 = 0.375. For a uniform, probability is just the fraction of the interval's length. ∎

Why n − 1 for a sample variance (trap #14)? You already used the data to estimate the mean, consuming one degree of freedom. Dividing by n would systematically under-estimate the true variance; n − 1 corrects that bias. GATE expects the population formula unless the word “sample” appears.

PROBLEM 4.9 · 2 marks

For {4, 8, 6, 5, 3, 8, 9, 1} find the mean, median, mode, population variance and both standard deviations.

Show full solution

Sorted: 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 8, 9 (n = 8).

Mean = 44/8 = 5.5. Median = (5+6)/2 = 5.5. Mode = 8.

Variance via the shortcut, with Σx² = 296:

σ² = 296/8 − 5.5² = 37 − 30.25 = 6.75

Verified both by the definition and by the E[X²] − μ² shortcut — they agree.

Population SD = √6.75 = 2.598076. Sample SD = √(8/7 × 6.75) = 2.774460.

The sample SD is larger, exactly as Bessel's correction guarantees. Mean = median here, indicating a roughly symmetric distribution. ∎
